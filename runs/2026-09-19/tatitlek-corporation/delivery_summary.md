## The outcome, and the thing that makes this study worth reading

They already run a capture tool, and the study's headline is a map of what it
already does, handed over free.

Four of the eight duty lines on their own requisition come back **Covered** by
GovEagle, two **Partly**, one **Unverified, ask your rep**, and data calls appear
on none of the four GovEagle pages we read.

**That map killed the build the engineering room set out to recommend.** The
strategist and the feasibility engineer both picked a Key Personnel and Past
Performance Evidence Engine. The engineer's first honest flag was that the whole
pick rested on an unretired assumption about GovEagle's feature set, retirable by
one public page fetch nobody had made, and it named the cost of being wrong.
The showrunner made the fetch. GovEagle's own pages already map task areas to
past performance and draft from past-performance write-ups. The build was a
duplicate of software they pay for, aimed at the VP whose own requisition names
it. The engineer re-ran on corrected facts and the pick became a measurement
rather than a tool.

## The one build we recommend

A free GovEagle coverage map, then one measured data call, then a deterministic
Data Call Tracker they own. **No model sits in the shipped path.** Rules, a
scheduler and a completeness board read against a compliance matrix GovEagle
already produces. It never authors a fact and never writes into a submitted
volume. We do not ask for an exclusive build, the tracker should end up hosted
and owned by GeoNorth, their own information-systems subsidiary. A company that
owns one would be insulted by any other position.

## The honest ROI

**The ask is eight to twelve thousand dollars for a measurement, not the build.**

The full five year path does NOT clear conservatively. 14 percent of a $126,816
five year cost at conservative assumptions, 67 percent at most likely, and only
the aggressive column pays back, at month 27. Recovered hours, 43 and 204 and
538 a year, not cash. Every driver except the $130,000 posted salary floor is
marked `assumed`, because nothing about their data calls is published and we
refuse to invent it.

The study says this in its own section heading: *The build does not pay back
under our conservative or our most likely assumptions, so we are not asking you
to fund the build.* The honest return of the ask is decision data, a measured
baseline that gates any further spend, and gate one is written so a good result
ends the engagement.
