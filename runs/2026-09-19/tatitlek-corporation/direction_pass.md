# THE DIRECTION PASS, 2026-09-19, The Tatitlek Corporation

One question, asked of the whole study and nothing else. **Does this make them
look worse than the evidence actually does?**

Three tests on every unflattering sentence. Could we KNOW this from outside their
building? Is the unflattering reading load-bearing, or decoration? Would they
recognise themselves, or get defensive at something they know is unfair?

## THE FINDING, and it is the whole reason this step exists

**The entire Today column of the before/after figure was invented, and it
contradicts the study's own honesty position three sections later.**

The study says plainly, twice, that we can't see how their data calls run and
that nothing we read says they are slow. Then the figure depicts their current
process step by step, in detail, unflatteringly: chasing by memory, keeping a
private list, rebuilding the picture from an inbox, discovering at the cutoff
that somebody never saw the request.

Every one of those is a guess wearing a finding's clothes. Not one is licensed
by anything in claims.json. A reader who reaches the honest section and reads
"nothing we read says they are slow" after looking at that figure has caught us
contradicting ourselves, and the figure is the most looked-at thing on the page.

This is the pipeline's incentive gradient doing exactly what the contract warns
it does. A before/after figure NEEDS a bad "before" to be persuasive, so the
before got written to be persuasive rather than to be true. The fact-checker
caught the same lean five times in the research package. It came back in the
figure, in a form no regex could see, which is precisely what this step is for.

## What was changed, and how

The figure is not deleted, because the before/after ledger is the document's
primary visual and the spec is right that it outranks the architecture diagram.
What changed is what it CLAIMS.

The left column is relabelled from "Today" to "A data call, as it generally
runs", and the note now says in the reader's first line that the left column is
the ordinary shape of this work and NOT a claim about how theirs runs, because
we can't see that from outside, and that the measurement exists to replace it
with their own numbers. The individual rows lose the pejorative colour, so
"chase by memory, around everything else in the week" becomes the neutral fact
that chasing and the outstanding count live wherever the coordinator keeps them.

That keeps the figure doing its real job, showing what work moves, while
removing every assertion we could not make.

## The accumulation check

Reading the whole study once more for tone rather than for sentences. With the
figure corrected, the unflattering weight now falls where the evidence is: on
the vendor market that would sell them a duplicate, and on our own ROI table,
which is the column that argues against our build. Nothing left in the document
tells this company it is behind. The study's posture is that they are further
ahead than we expected and that the honest gap is small, which is what the
verified record actually supports.

One sentence deliberately left standing: "The expensive mistake here is not
moving slowly, it is buying the same capability twice." It is unflattering in
prospect, it is load-bearing, and it is the finding. Softening it would leave
the study with no point.

## claim_sweep judgments, recorded rather than silently passed

claim_sweep is a REPORT and the judgment is the showrunner's. Two CONFLICTS
stand at ship time and both are deliberate.

- `[percent] 14 vs 67`. The conservative and most likely recovery shares. Three

- `[percent] 45 vs 90`. Two different Deltek statistics, 90 percent of

  They are the adoption figure and its honest counterweight, they are attributed
  to different sources in the same sentence, and carrying both together is the
  point. Not a conflict.

Thirteen ECHOES, all body-against-sources restatements or the brief restating
the body, which FIELD_STUDY_SPEC requires so the brief survives being forwarded
alone. No QUOTE DRIFT and no CONDITIONAL MISMATCH at any round.

## Round two of the study-critic found a SECOND direction, and it is the rarer one

Round one's drift was the usual one, the study reaching inside their building.
Round two's was the opposite and more flattering to us: seven small errors all
inflating our own concession, an unverified ninth duty on the coverage map, a
dropped partial row, two figure rows struck as wholly gone when the ROI removes
a quarter, an attribution one step closer to primary than the evidence, a
percentage computed across the diagonal, and a demo asserting an export the
study itself calls unverified.

Worth writing down because it is not the failure mode this routine watches for.
The study's whole trust play is "we killed our own build", and the incentive
gradient bends toward making that concession look cleaner than it was earned.
A study that overstates its own honesty is still overstating something, and the
reader who runs GovEagle can check the map row by row.
