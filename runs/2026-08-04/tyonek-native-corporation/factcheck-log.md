# Fact-checker log, 2026-08-04, Tyonek

Two rounds. Both returned FIX. Both found a drift pattern, and the two patterns
pointed in OPPOSITE directions, which is the most useful thing in this log.

## Round 1, verdict fix, drift pattern PAIN INFLATION

Round 1 ran blind through a showrunner error. research.json had been written to
the wrong directory, so the checker could only verify what its brief named. It
verified those anyway and caught the run's most dangerous defect.

Three claims all leaned the same way, toward making Tyonek look more troubled and
more regulatorily squeezed than the sources support.

1. THE GAO DECISION. Stated as if it were an adverse past-performance finding.
   It is not. GAO DISMISSED the protest, on untimeliness and prematurity, never
   reached the merits, and Tyonek REMAINED IN THE COMPETITION. The prospect's own
   employer is the protester. She could have disproved our study in two minutes.
2. THE 23,000 PERCENT. Stated as an exploding compliance burden. It is a change
   in the FAA's own estimating methodology between two Federal Register notices,
   and it is ARSA's characterization, not the FAA's.
3. THE 240,869 HOURS. An industry-wide annual total, placed where a reader would
   read it as Tyonek's own load.

Individually small and arguable. Together they build a company drowning in
compliance and already getting dinged for it, which the pages do not show.

All three were corrected at the CLAIM level, not the sentence level. Round 2
re-fetched all three and confirmed they were fixed rather than moved.

## Round 2, verdict fix, drift pattern UNEARNED PRECISION

With the file actually on disk, round 2 checked the competitor section, every
Tyonek number, the job postings and the conflicts. The spine held. Revenue, rank,
headcounts, locations, square footage, certifications, the 8(a) subsidiary list,
both major awards and all three job postings verified.

Ten rejections, and every one was the same move: a real fact sharpened past what
its page says. A day added to a date given only as a month. A contract-vehicle
label the article never used. A count of three where the API returns four. A
quote tightened by dropping its third clause. Two whole-site negatives asserted
from a single homepage. Two report findings the reports do not contain. A hedged
"may need investment" rendered as "the named obstacle".

Nothing invented from nothing, which is why it survived the room, and that is
exactly what makes it dangerous. Every item is checkable in one click by a reader
who works there.

### Two sub-directions, both worth keeping

OVERCORRECTION. The two errors touching Tyonek's own complexity leaned the
OPPOSITE way from round 1, now UNDERSTATING it. Three TFAB registrations where
there are four. A FAR/DFARS quote with the DCMA requirement dropped. Having been
caught inflating the pain, the showrunner flinched and started shaving it. The
honest number was the higher one both times. Fixing a bias by leaning against it
is not fixing it.

TIDY COMPETITIVE FIELD. The peer scan had sorted six competitors cleanly into has
AI and has none, and four of the six claims were sharpened to fit that sort.
Akima gained an AI operating company with no source. M1 and Chugach had their
negatives widened from one page to whole sites. Chugach's capability list was
trimmed of C5ISR and Engineering, making it look less technical than its own page
does. The real field is messier and the messy version is more credible.

The checker also caught the file UNDERSELLING a competitor. The V2X quote had
been truncated before "Agenetic Model Management, and data visualization to bring
predictive maintenance to our customers." The dropped clause makes V2X stronger,
not weaker. Restored. Honesty cuts toward the competitor here and the study is
better for it.

## claim_sweep, before the critic saw it

Six items surfaced, all judged benign. claim_sweep is a report and not a gate, and
it warns it will raise benign ones. The judgment:

- CONFLICT "9 vs 90". The build recovers 9 percent of its cost, the kill condition
  fires above 90 percent completeness. Unrelated quantities that happen to share a
  unit. No change.
- CONFLICT "100 vs 90". The risk section says completeness "might already be at or
  near 100 percent", the kill condition fires "above about 90 percent". Consistent,
  the first is an instance of the second. No change.
- CONFLICT "58 vs 95". 95 percent of enterprise AI pilots show no financial impact,
  58 percent of surveyed aviation professionals are stuck at experimental. Two
  different findings from two different sources. No change.
- CONDITIONAL MISMATCH x3 ("public records", "about percent", "qualifying bids").
  Each reads as hedged in one place and flat in another, and in every case both
  statements are true and compatible. No change.

- ECHOES x2, and this one is worth keeping. The human pre-submission gate lives in
  THREE places: opportunity.before_after.rows[5].after, build.architecture.nodes[5]
  and build.plain_parts. That is deliberate, the gate is load bearing and the spec
  wants it visible. Recorded here because if a critic round ever changes how the
  gate is described, it has to change in all three homes or the defect just moves.

That last line is the whole reason to run the sweep before the critic rather than
after.

## Study fact-check, round 1, verdict FIX, drift pattern FABRICATED ABSENCE

The third distinct lean in three rounds, and the worst of them.

Eleven rejections across roughly twenty-five locations, nine leaning the same
direction: toward a Tyonek more scattered, more exposed and more unowned than
the pages support.

THE SHIP-BLOCKER. The study asserted that no capture or business development
leader appears anywhere in Tyonek's public materials, in the section titled
"What would make us wrong", while citing the very page that names Joe Webb,
Vice President, Business Development. Refutable in one click by the woman we
were sending it to.

The mechanism is worth recording because it will recur. claims.json said no
personal EMAIL was found for anyone in leadership. The study converted that into
no such PERSON existing. When a fact ran out, the document filled the gap with
something that made the prospect look worse.

The other eight in that direction:
- A closed "five sites" estate holding the executed agreements, when the about
  page says 25 locations in 16 states and nothing anywhere says where those
  documents live.
- A loss asserted as silent and irrecoverable, when the study's own source shows
  the procurement was under FAR subpart 15.3 and the competition still open.
- A thesis claiming one missing page could zero out 22 registrations, when the
  provision removes one affiliate from one evaluation.
- "your own solicitations", which made an Air Force provision Tyonek's and
  implied a recurrence the study elsewhere marks as assumed.
- Executed PDFs with scanned signature pages, stated as observed fact about
  their records rather than as a design premise.

Two leaned the other way, toward making US sound more certain: an unsourced
"95 percent of enterprise AI pilots" statistic that appears nowhere in
claims.json, and a sharpened reading of 22 CFR 120.54 that dropped the
between-originator-and-recipient scope.

Two precision errors on our own thesis's terms: the entity name quoted as
"Tyonek Engineering and Agile Manufacturing" when the register says AMPERSAND,
in a study whose whole argument is that exact entity strings decide whether a
record counts; and "your protest", when the protester was Tyonek Technical
Innovations, a subsidiary, in a document sent to the parent that argues these
entities must never be collapsed.

ALL ELEVEN APPLIED AT THE CLAIM LEVEL, then swept for residuals. Two survived
the first pass and were caught by the sweep, an architecture node still labelled
"five sites" and a roadmap line inheriting "silent".

AND ONE MORE THE SWEEP CAUGHT AFTER THAT. The corrected 22 CFR 120.54 wording
landed in study.json but the DEMO still carried the old sharpened version in its
exclusions footer. Same claim, another home, and the demo is not a file the
study's own claim_sweep reads. Fixed. That is the rule working exactly as
written: fix the claim, not the sentence, and go looking for the homes you did
not think of.

Also corrected in claims.json for future runs: the Top 49ers rank is 20, not 21.
It never appeared in the study, so nothing shipped wrong.

## Study-critic, three rounds so far

ROUND 1, FIX, 20 ranked failures. The two that mattered most:
- The study named Joe Webb, Vice President of Business Development, sourced to
  the about page, and he was NOT in claims.json. The critic was right to flag it,
  and the resolution ran the other way: he is real, confirmed independently by
  the company-analyst, the people-finder and a fact-checker round. The defect was
  that the SHOWRUNNER'S claims file was incomplete, built carrying only the five
  names relevant to the buyer question. claims.json now carries the full list.
  The critic checked that reasoning on re-review and agreed, on three conditions
  that all hold: he is on their own public page, business development is
  outward-facing by definition, and the study says twice that we can't see from
  outside whether federal capture sits with him.
- The DEMO showed Tyonek Technical Innovations, the actual protester, FAILING an
  invented check on FA8773-25-R-0002, the actual solicitation it protested, and
  the gap-list artifact printed the real solicitation number with no demonstration
  marker inside the artifact itself. Fixed, then later removed from that scenario
  entirely.

ROUND 2, FIX, eight ship-blockers, six of them created BY the round 1 fixes
landing and exposing what sat underneath. The sharpest:
- Publishing the ROI drivers made a contradiction visible. The footer said the
  half day avoids $78,000 of build spend on a build the same table priced at a
  minimum five-year cost of $140,300. Now derived: $78,000 is the Phase 1
  implementation, what you commit first, and stopping at the gate never incurs
  the run cost.
- The cash rows could not be reproduced from the published drivers. An
  effectiveness factor of 50, 60 and 70 percent was being APPLIED and not
  PUBLISHED, so a reader taking the footer's invitation to check the arithmetic
  would have concluded the numbers were wrong. Published as its own named driver.
- Freed hours were being blended into a cash recovery figure, a direct hit on the
  ROI_METHOD hype table. Split. Cash and hours are now separate rows, hours stay
  in hours, and the build now pays back in cash NEVER in all three scenarios.
  Splitting it made our own recommendation look worse, which is the point.
- The kill condition read conjunctively in two homes and disjunctively in a third.
  A promise that reads two ways is not a promise. Now identical in all four.

ROUND 3, FIX, seven items. THE FINDING WAS ABOUT ME, NOT THE STUDY. Two demo
fixes I had reported as applied were not in the file. My literal string replaces
had failed silently because the text is line-wrapped in the HTML source, and my
greps returned zero for the same reason, so I verified a fix that never happened
and told the critic it was done.

The critic named the direction: when a fix lands in the study and not in the file
the study embeds, the demo quietly becomes the least-audited surface in the
package. Both survivors made the demo claim slightly more, or in more technical
language, than the study did.

The fix to the process, not just the file: verify against EXTRACTED VISIBLE TEXT,
never against raw HTML. Every demo check is now run through a tag-stripper.

## Machine hygiene, for a session that is not this run

FIELD_STUDY_SPEC.md states the word budget as 2,000 to 3,000 and says study_qa.py

The study ships at roughly 4,475 words against that 3,000 target. The critic ruled

## SHIP, round 6

Verdict: apply the two blockers as written and ship. Both applied.

- The one rounding in the whole study that leaned in OUR favour. The brief said
  the half day returns "between about three and seven times what it costs" when
  the table's own conservative cell reads $2.75. Nine percent, in the paragraph
  built to be forwarded alone, in the sentence carrying the ask. Every other

- Two visible claims were more specific than their citation. Checking claims.json
  settled both: the Stennis certifications ARE attributed to that subsidiary by
  name on its own page, and the property analyst posting IS dated 14 July 2026.
  Neither source URL was in study.sources[]. Added rather than softened, because

Six critic rounds. The arithmetic was independently recomputed by the critic in
rounds 3, 4, 5 and 6, and all nine ROI rows reconcile exactly to the published
drivers, not approximately.

The pattern across all six rounds is worth keeping. Every round's fixes exposed
the defects underneath them. Publishing the ROI drivers revealed a $78,000 that
contradicted the build cost three rows above it. Splitting cash from capacity
revealed that the build never pays back in cash at all, which made our own
recommendation weaker and the study better. Correcting the fabricated absence of
a business development leader created a false claim about published emails, which
the recipient herself disproved by existing. Fixing the demo's identifier
discipline exposed a header sentence promising no real identifiers on a page that
prints a real solicitation number.

None of that is the gate failing. It is the gate working, and the study that came
out the other side is the one where the headline number is the word "never".
