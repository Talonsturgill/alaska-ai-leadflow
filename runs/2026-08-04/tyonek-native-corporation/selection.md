## DROPPED AT THE PHASE 1 VALUES SCREEN

The ANC scout was briefed to screen this segment hard, because it has burned us
before. It returned five names it refused to put forward, and it found three of
them on its own rather than reading them off the exclude list.

- **Cape Fox Corporation** (capefoxcorp.com). ProPublica documented fraud and
  self-dealing alleged in five of its seven 8(a) companies. Independently
  verified before recording.
- **The Eyak Corporation** (eyakcorporation.com). Reported False Claims Act and
  Anti-Kickback settlement by its subsidiaries.
- **Chenega Corporation** (chenega.com). NOT SUPPRESSED, and this one is worth
  writing down. The scout reported detention operation services and cited a
  third-party contract aggregator, and it also claimed chenega.com was already
  on the exclude list, which it was not. The showrunner checked, and what
  surfaced was armed security at IRS facilities and at the DHS Federal Law
  Enforcement Training Centers, which is federal facility guarding, not ICE
  detention. Two errors on one item, so the item was distrusted and no
  suppression was recorded. A reason we can't prove does not go in the ledger.
  If Chenega surfaces as a candidate on a future run it needs a real check, not
  this note and not that aggregator page.
- **Afognak Native Corporation** (afognak.com). Parent of Alutiiq, LLC, already
  suppressed. The subsidiary rule runs upward too.
- **Chugach Alaska Corporation** (chugach.com). Reported False Claims Act
  settlement over a billing misstatement on a military construction project.

A note the room should hold onto. Koniag is a Kodiak-region corporation and so
is Afognak Native Corporation, whose subsidiary Alutiiq LLC is suppressed. They
are SEPARATE ENTITIES and one's record is not the other's. The research room was
briefed explicitly not to conflate them.

## WHY NOT THE OTHERS

Tourism has been served five times now (Camp Denali, Alaska Wildland, Allen
Marine, North Country Charters, Big Dan's) and the scout was asked for different
shapes, which it delivered. It still lost on the calendar. A tour operator in the
first week of August is at peak operating load with no attention to spare, while
a federal contractor in August is staring straight at September 30.
