# THE DIRECTION PASS, 2026-08-06, Anchorage Neighborhood Health Center

One question, asked of the whole study and nothing else.
**Does this make them look worse than the evidence actually does?**

The fact-checker found a two-directional drift in the research package before
the study existed. Half of it pushed toward making ANHC look more troubled than
the record supports. This pass exists because that gradient does not switch off
just because someone named it.

Every sentence that asserts something unflattering, what licenses it, and the
verdict.

---

## 1. "Your front door still ends in a callback." (thesis)

LICENSED BY: C10 and C11, ANHC's own published new patient page, verbatim, twice.

COULD WE KNOW IT FROM OUTSIDE? Yes. They publish it themselves.

LOAD-BEARING OR DECORATION? Load-bearing. It is the whole finding.

WOULD THEY RECOGNISE THEMSELVES? Yes, and the first half of the sentence gives
them the credit that makes the second half land as observation rather than
insult. **KEEP.**

## 2. "Two published waits, and a competitor across town that books on screen."

LICENSED BY: C10, C11 and C80, all primary pages, all re-fetched.

CHECK: the risk is that it reads as ANHC being beaten. The body already says
"We are describing your published service level, not a failure" and "A one to
two business day promise is a real commitment and plenty of clinics publish
nothing at all", and body_2 says Providence "is a large system with a different
balance sheet." That is three separate softenings on one comparison, which is
the right density for the sharpest claim in the document. **KEEP.**

## 3. "nobody can say what the loop costs, including us"

LICENSED BY: absence of any published figure, after a real search.

THE TEST THIS SECTION HAD TO PASS: could we actually KNOW this from outside their
building? No, and the study says so in exactly those terms: "We could find no
published measure". It does NOT say "ANHC does not measure this", which would be
a guess wearing a finding's clothes. The phrase "including us" puts us in the
same boat rather than above them. **KEEP, as written, and the wording is the
point.**

## 4. The 53,021 versus 47,783 visit-count mismatch

VERDICT: **CUT, and it was cut before the study was drafted.**

This was the single highest-risk sentence available about this company. Both
figures are verified (C27, C21) and the temptation to print them side by side was
real, because it looks like rigour. It is not. Neither carries a period label,
the metric names differ, and any reader would hear "your numbers went down."
The discovery map flagged that raising it "risks reading as a scold rather than
a service." It appears nowhere in the study. Not in the brief, not in what we
found, not in the sources list.

## 5. The FY2023 deficit

VERDICT: **CUT.** Verified (C42) and three years stale, followed by two surplus
years. Using it to characterise their present finances would be false. The study
mentions no deficit anywhere. The ROI section takes the opposite and accurate
reading, that the money exists and the PRICING SHAPE is the constraint.

## 6. "At least fifteen roles open" and anything about staffing

VERDICT: **CUT.** C89 is a same-day job board snapshot with no trend behind it
and may not be used to claim turnover or growth, and the headcount claim was
killed outright. The study prints no role count and no staff count. The only
thing carried over is the posted wage band, which is used as a costing anchor
rather than as evidence of strain.

## 7. The discontinued mammography service (C26)

VERDICT: **CUT from the argument.** It is verified, recent and dated, and it
would have made an easy hook. It is also a service change they announced
themselves while stating they remain committed to screening through referral.
Using a service closure as evidence of trouble is decoration, not a finding, and
it is not load-bearing for the intake build. It appears nowhere.

## 8. The 2 to 3 week wait to establish care (C88)

VERDICT: **CUT.** An outside op-ed writer, October 2024, nearly two years stale,
and the same sentence records a 10 day actual experience. Printing the 2 to 3
weeks without the 10 days would be exactly the drift this pass exists to catch,
and printing both costs more words than the point is worth. Absent.

## 9. "the best evidenced use anywhere, drew a high degree of success from only 53 percent"

LICENSED BY: C68, verified.

DIRECTION CHECK: this is unflattering about THE FIELD, not about ANHC, and it
cuts against our own sale. That is the correct direction for a caveat to point.
**KEEP.**

## 10. The whole "What would make us wrong" section

Four items, every one of them a way ANHC should NOT buy this. The strongest is
that the automatic send might move nothing at all, where the study commits in
advance to writing up a recommendation against the second phase. That is us
looking worse, not them. **KEEP.**

---

## THE ACCUMULATION TEST

Reading the eight unflattering candidates above, five were cut and three kept.
The three kept are all things ANHC published about themselves. Every cut was a
case where we would have been inferring something about the inside of their
building from the outside.

The compounding check: do the survivors, read together, add up to a portrait of
an organisation in trouble? No, and the reason is structural rather than lucky.
The study OPENS on what they got right, and it is not a courtesy line. C34 is a
verified fact that reframes the whole document: they put AI into clinical
production ahead of every Alaska peer we checked, before anyone approached them.
The thesis, the brief and the build section all carry it. A reader who stops
after the first screen has been told they are ahead, not behind.

## THE ONE THING THAT STILL COULD LAND WRONG

The comparison with Providence. It is verified, it is the sharpest thing in the
document, and it is the only place where somebody else is doing something ANHC
is not. It survives because the alternative is to hide the one fact that makes
the finding matter. It carries three softenings and it never says ANHC is
behind. Watch for the study-critic disagreeing, and if it does, soften rather
than defend.

## WHAT THIS PASS CHANGED

Nothing, this round. Every cut listed above was made during assembly, with the
fact-checker's drift_pattern paragraph open beside the draft. That is the pass
working upstream rather than the pass finding nothing, and the distinction is
worth recording: the artifact is the gate, and the gate was applied while the
study was written rather than after.

## ADDENDUM, WRITTEN AFTER THE FACT-CHECKER CAME BACK

This pass concluded "nothing changed this round." That was wrong, and the
correction matters more than the original pass does.

The fact-checker, reading the same study, found a residual lean I did not, in
the same direction the earlier drift had run. Four sentences asserted that the
1 to 2 business day window is STAFF QUEUE TIME. What is actually verified is
only that ANHC publishes a window and never says what is inside it. The study's
own honest section conceded exactly that, so the document was stating the
sharper version in the body and the honest version at the end.

The same claim had SIX homes across found, opportunity, costing and build. That
is precisely the failure mode the run contract warns about: a fix applied to one
sentence would have read as fixed while the study still said it. All six were
corrected together.

Two more of the same family went with them. "We have no reason to think they sit
in a report either" asserted something about a private system we can't see, and
"the clinical side got instrumented and then it got better" upgraded an operator
anecdote into evidence of measurement, which the earlier pass had explicitly
ruled out.

WHAT THIS SAYS ABOUT THE PASS ITSELF. Reading for tone with the draft in front of
me was not enough to catch a claim I had written and believed. The fact-checker
caught it by asking a narrower question, what does the fetched page actually say,
and finding the gap between that and the sentence. The lesson for this artifact
is that the direction pass is stronger when it tests each unflattering sentence
against the source text rather than against my own sense of fairness, and that a
pass which reports finding nothing should be treated as suspicious rather than
reassuring.
