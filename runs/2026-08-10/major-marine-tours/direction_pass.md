# THE DIRECTION PASS, 2026-08-10, Major Marine Tours

One question, asked of the whole study and nothing else.

**Does this make them look worse than the evidence actually does?**

This run has already had that drift named twice by other gates, so this pass is
looking for what survived them. The fact-checker called it a *sharpening reflex*:
every quote the research package rebuilt from memory got rebuilt in the direction
of the point it was supporting. The feasibility engineer found the same lean in the
strategist's timeline, which dated a two-season programme against one March gate and
made the prospect look readier than the evidence supports.

The three questions, applied to every sentence that asserts something unflattering.

---

## 1. "Your rules are exact, and they are spread across three pages and one person's memory"

**What licenses it.** c1 through c20, all verbatim from their own pages. The three
refund clocks are on one page, the shuttle rule on another, accessibility on the FAQ.

**Could we know this from outside their building?** Yes, entirely. We read the pages.
The claim is about *where the rules are published*, not about what anyone at Major
Marine remembers or fails to remember.

**KEEP**, with one word checked. "One person's memory" is the only part that reaches
inside the building. It survives because it is framed as the alternative to a page,
not as a criticism of anyone. The heading's first clause does real work here, "your
rules are exact" is the honest half and it leads.

---

## 2. "Three of these four questions have nobody downstream to catch a wrong answer"

**What licenses it.** Structural inference from their own published process, not an
observation of their staff. A refund error surfaces after money moves. An
accessibility error surfaces at the gangway. A shuttle error surfaces in a parking
lot at 7:30 a.m.

**Could we know this from outside?** Yes. It follows from the published policies plus
the absence of any published verification step. We are not claiming errors happen. We
are claiming that *if* one happens, nothing published catches it.

**KEEP.** This is load-bearing, it is the actual finding, and it is stated as a
property of the process rather than of the people.

---

## 3. "The person answering the phone is often somebody hired inside your January-to-April window who is in their first weeks"

**What licenses it.** c24, their own employment page. About 60 seasonal employees,
peak hiring January 15th to April 15th, Customer Service and Sales Staff named first
among the roles.

**Is the unflattering reading load-bearing or decoration?** Load-bearing. It is the
whole reason the build is aimed where it is.

**Would they recognise themselves?** Yes. This is a fact they publish about
themselves and it is ordinary for a seasonal operator.

**KEEP**, and the next sentence earns it: *"They are not failing to read."* That
sentence exists specifically to stop the paragraph reading as a complaint about
staff quality. It stays.

---

## 4. The four website contradictions

**What licenses it.** c36, c37, plus the Orca Quest index omission and the Rachel
Marie discrepancy. All verified.

**SOFTENED, and this is the most important call in this pass.** These are *website
defects*, and OUTREACH_CRAFT bans them as an opener and the binding constraints ban
them as a headline. In the study they appear in exactly one place, `build_vs_buy`,
and only as the reason retrieval is not funded yet: search can't resolve a
contradiction, so the corpus has to be reconciled first.

The framing is deliberately ordinary. Any site of that age accumulates this. What
was cut: any sentence characterising the site as neglected, any count presented as
alarming, and any implication that these defects are costing them money today. The
study never says "your website is a mess" and never implies it.

---

## 5. "A guest walks to the harbour for a sailing they will miss"

**What licenses it.** c13, c14, c15, c16 and c9. Every link is their published rule.

**Could we know this from outside?** Yes, every step is published.

**KEEP, and note what was CUT beside it.** The study says plainly that this costs
them *goodwill and not revenue*, because a no-show is non-refundable and the company
keeps the fare. Presenting it as lost revenue would have been the single most
tempting inflation available in this whole study, and it is refused in the text
itself rather than quietly omitted. That refusal is doing honesty work in the
prospect's favour and it stays visible.

---

## 6. The ROI section

**This is where the direction runs the OTHER way, and that is worth recording.**

Every previous run of this desk drifted toward making the prospect look worse. This
study's largest correction went the opposite direction: the conservative case did
not clear at 18,000 dollars, so the ask was cut to 4,500 and the failure appears in
the section *heading*. Nothing in the ROI section makes them look bad. It makes
*our own first estimate* look wrong, which is accurate.

Two disclosures survive that most proposals would bury. That the 4,500 figure was
arrived at by solving for the ceiling the conservative benefit supports and then
cutting scope to fit under it, which is exactly the reverse-engineering Flyvbjerg
warns about. And that we design the table that defines a "miss" while benefiting
from a longer miss list.

**KEEP both.**

---

## 7. What was CUT before it reached the study

- **The rail check-in finding.** The strongest-looking pain in the dossier, killed on
  a footnote we had not read. It would have told them their own check-in rule was
  broken when their page already solves it.
- **Any implication that guests complain.** Google sits at 4.9 from 2,555 reviews and
  not one verbatim negative review could be fetched. No guest sentiment appears
  anywhere in the study, in either direction.
- **"Two-port operator" and every Whittier reference.**
- **The self-serve cancellation gap**, which is field-wide including the NYSE-listed
  competitor. Writing it as their deficiency would have been false and unfair.
- **Phone-only packages as a defect.** Scoped nothing against it and said why: it is
  at least as consistent with a deliberate margin decision.

---

## VERDICT

**No sentence in this study asserts something unflattering that we could not know
from outside their building, and none is decoration.**

The accumulation test, which is the half a regex can't see: read end to end, the
study reads as *this is a well-run company with a structural timing problem it did
not create*. The season resets the desk every spring. That is not a failure, it is
the shape of a seasonal business in Alaska, and the study says so.

The single strongest signal that the direction is right this time: **the study's
headline recommendation is that they spend a quarter of what we first proposed, and
its most prominent number is our own conservative case failing.**
