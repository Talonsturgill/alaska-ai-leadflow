# The locked pick, 2026-08-05, Fountainhead Hotels

## What is locked

**The Desk Answer Book.** A canonical, property-aware, season-aware answer set for
the front desk, owned by them, with a deterministic season flip, a printable shift
sheet, a named owner and a change log. **No AI in it.**

**Desk Search** (semantic matching that returns their own card verbatim, one
model-touched step, property and season as deterministic pre-filters) is named as a
**conditional Next that is not priced today**. It is funded only if the Phase-1
query log proves the gap is real.

The ai-feasibility-engineer overruled the strategist's provisional pick and it was
right to. The strategist's own RICE table scored the zero-AI candidate at 4.05
against the AI candidate at 2.1, nearly double, and then picked the AI one on a
higher-ceiling argument resting on a single invented anecdote. Three things decided
the downscope:

1. **The compounding math alone kills the generation step.** Seven model-touched
   steps at the illustrative per-step figures in AI_SCOPING lands near 70 percent
   end to end, and near 48 percent at 90 percent per step. Desk Search removes four
   of those steps by construction, because property is a login setting and the
   season is a date, both deterministic and therefore right by definition.
2. **The human in the loop was partly a comfortable story.** The reviewer is by
   design a first-week seasonal hire, which is the person in the building least able
   to detect a wrong answer, because not knowing the answer is why they asked. The
   loop is real for consequence and weak for detection. A wrongly matched card is
   visibly about the wrong thing. A wrongly composed sentence is not.
3. **The name was doing dishonest work.** A phase two that might never be funded
   was becoming the headline, and in our own pipeline a pick named Copilot produces
   a demo that is an AI chat box, which would contradict the study's own conclusion.

## Cagan's four risks, checked for THIS owner

**Feasibility.** Cleared, and trivially. Rules, dates and editorial work. Nothing
here needs a model to be right, which is the whole point. The engineer walked the
ladder and the lowest rung serves the job.

**Value.** Real but modest, and it must be sold at that size. This is not a rescue.
They already publish a codified FAQ and staff three desks around the clock, which is
why the strategist scored current satisfaction at 4 rather than 0. What the build
beats is the three cases where turning your head fails, the veteran is on the phone,
in the back, or is himself a seasonal hire that week. The honest observation the
study will carry is that the highest-adoption artifact here is probably the printed
shift sheet taped to the desk, not the search box.

**Usability.** Cleared. A card set, a season toggle and a sheet of paper. The
adoption bar is that it must be faster than turning your head, which is a design
constraint we state rather than a risk we hide.

**Business viability.** Cleared, and it is what sizes the ask. A family-held
independent founded in 1985 buys a small, staged, stoppable engagement. It does not
buy a platform. The build reinforces what they say they are, truly local and
independent, and it explicitly declines to sell them a me-too version of a chain
mobile key.

**Four Forces re-run.** Push and pull clear anxiety and habit, but narrowly, and
only because the build stays behind the desk. Habit is the strongest opposing force
and the study says so out loud.

## The fatal unknown, moved ahead of pricing

Whether the seasonal front-of-house roster is mostly **returning** staff or mostly
first-time hires. The company says it more than doubles hotel staffing for summer
and publishes no split. If the roster is largely returning, the time-to-competence
framing collapses and any onboarding-days return goes with it.

It resizes the prize sharply and does not zero it, because returning staff return
after roughly eight months away across a product switch, the aurora-date
inconsistency on their own pages is a content defect regardless of who reads it, and
a canonical answer set still serves year-round staff and the general inbox.

One email retires it. It is asked in the first conversation, **before** anything is
scoped or priced, and the study says that plainly rather than discovering it during
a paid phase.

## ECONOMICS PRE-CHECK (before the engineering room spends)

**The ask being sized: roughly 18,000 to 26,000 dollars, fixed fee, Phase 1 only.**
The range is driven by one named unknown, how much operating knowledge is already
written down versus sitting in a veteran's head. Weeks of work, not quarters.

**What honest return can this ask clear?** Not a labour-savings number. Every input
one would need is missing and we may not invent any of them. There is no headcount
we may use, no time-to-competence baseline, no escalation rate, no call volume, and
the one strong independent measurement in the package cannot be rescaled onto
third-party room counts.

So the return is **decision data plus a durable artifact**, which the contract
explicitly permits as an honest pilot shape. Concretely:

1. An answer set they own permanently and keep whether or not they ever spend
   another dollar with us, with the aurora-date inconsistency resolved.
2. Four baselines that do not exist today, measured through the August 21 season
   flip, which decide whether anything further is worth funding.

**How the conservative case clears its own bar.** The ROI section will not project a
benefit it cannot source. It will state a **break-even threshold** instead, how many
supervisor interruptions per shift this would have to prevent to pay for itself, and
invite them to check that against their own desk in a week. A threshold is
falsifiable, a projection here would be fiction. Every driver is labelled assumed,
and the table will carry no `verified` row about their business, which
FIELD_STUDY_SPEC explicitly calls a fine and honest outcome because it tells the
reader that nothing in it is yet a fact about their business, which is exactly what
a pilot is for.

**Verdict: the ask clears.** A small fixed fee returning a permanent operational
artifact plus the measurements that gate every later decision is defensible at this
size. It would NOT be defensible at platform prices, and it is not being asked at
platform prices. Proceed to the engineering room on this ask, not on the biggest
build we could imagine.
